Understanding Ghana's Over-Age Penalty for Imported Vehicles

Published on: May 2026 | Category: Customs & Tax

When calculating the total budget for importing a used car into Ghana, the most critical factor buyers often overlook is the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) Over-Age Penalty. To protect the environment and reduce road accidents, the Ghanaian government imposes strict financial penalties on older vehicles entering the country. Understanding this rule is essential to maintaining your profit margins.

The 10-Year Rule Explained

According to the Customs Act of Ghana, any motor vehicle that is more than 10 years old from its date of manufacture attracts an over-age penalty. This penalty is calculated as a percentage of the CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) value of the vehicle. Therefore, a cheap 15-year-old car might end up costing you more in total landed cost than a slightly more expensive 8-year-old car.

Current Penalty Structure

While standard import duties (typically ranging from 5% to 20%), VAT, and NHIL still apply to all vehicles, the additional over-age penalties are structured progressively based on the vehicle's exact age:

  • Less than 10 years: 0% Penalty (Highly Recommended)
  • 10 to 12 years old: 5% of CIF Value
  • 12 to 15 years old: 20% of CIF Value
  • 15 to 20 years old: 50% of CIF Value
  • Over 20 years old: 100% of CIF Value

How to Strategize Your Purchase

At GK Trade, we strongly advise our Ghanaian partners to target vehicles manufactured between 2017 and 2020. For instance, importing a 2018 Hyundai Tucson ensures that you pay absolutely zero over-age penalties upon arrival at Tema Port. If your budget is tight and you must purchase an older model, we recommend staying strictly within the 10-12 year bracket (e.g., 2015 models) where the penalty is a manageable 5%.
